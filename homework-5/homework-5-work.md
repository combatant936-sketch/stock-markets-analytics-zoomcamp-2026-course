# Question 1: [Exploratory] Research Idea Number One

## Answer:

### Title: Using Google Search Trends to Predict Luxury Sports Car Stock Performance

1. Market and scope

I want to study publicly traded luxury sports car manufacturers, mainly Ferrari (NYSE: RACE) and Porsche AG (XETRA: P911). The research will focus on the global luxury sports car market, particularly the United States and Europe. My intended investment horizon is 1–3 months.

2. Investment thesis

My hypothesis is that increasing online interest in luxury sports car brands may indicate growing consumer demand. For example, an increase in Google searches for Ferrari and Porsche could indicate greater interest in their vehicles. I want to investigate whether this interest can predict future vehicle deliveries, company performance, or stock returns.

3. Potential edge

Financial reports usually provide information about company performance after a reporting period. Google Trends provides more frequent information about consumer search behavior. My potential edge would be identifying changes in consumer interest before they appear in quarterly financial results. However, online interest does not always translate into actual purchases.

4. Data and sources
- Google Trends: Free search-interest data for Ferrari and Porsche.
- Ferrari Investor Relations: Official financial reports, revenue, and vehicle deliveries.
- Porsche Investor Relations: Financial reports and vehicle delivery information.
- Stooq: Historical stock prices.
These sources are publicly accessible. I would still need to verify historical data coverage and download the relevant datasets before conducting the research.

5. Research approach

I would collect monthly Google search-interest data and historical stock prices for Ferrari and Porsche. I would compare changes in search interest with stock returns over the following 1–3 months. I would also compare search interest with quarterly vehicle deliveries to determine whether it provides an early indication of demand.

My proposed signal would be a consistent increase in search interest compared with the previous three-month average. I would investigate whether this signal could help identify potential buying opportunities.

6. Validation and risks

I would compare the strategy against a simple buy-and-hold strategy for Ferrari and Porsche. I would reject the idea if increases in search interest do not consistently predict future stock performance or vehicle deliveries.

Potential risks include misleading search activity caused by advertising or news, delays in financial reporting, and the possibility that stock prices already reflect consumer interest. Transaction costs could also reduce potential returns.


# Question 2: [Exploratory] Research Idea Number Two

## Answer:

### Title: Pairs Trading Between Ferrari and Porsche Stocks

1. Market and scope

I want to investigate two publicly traded luxury automobile manufacturers: Ferrari (NYSE: RACE) and Porsche AG (XETRA: P911). Both companies operate in the premium automobile industry but have different business models and product positioning. My intended investment horizon is 1–3 months.

2. Investment thesis

My hypothesis is that Ferrari and Porsche stocks may sometimes experience temporary differences in performance because of market reactions to company-specific news or changes in investor expectations.

For example, negative news about luxury vehicle demand might affect both companies, but one stock could decline more than the other. I want to investigate whether these differences sometimes reverse over time.

3. Potential edge

Investors may react differently to similar industry developments, creating temporary differences in the relative performance of related companies. My potential edge would be identifying unusually large differences in their historical relative performance and investigating whether they return toward their normal relationship.

However, I would first test whether a stable relationship exists because Ferrari and Porsche have different profitability, product portfolios, and customer segments.

4. Data and sources
- Stooq: Free historical stock-price data.
- Ferrari Investor Relations: Financial reports and company performance.
- Porsche Investor Relations: Financial reports and business information.
- SEC EDGAR: Free access to company filings.
These resources provide publicly available financial information. I would need to verify the historical price coverage for both exchanges before starting the analysis.

5. Research approach

I would collect daily historical prices for Ferrari and Porsche and calculate their daily percentage returns. Then I would calculate their relative performance and measure how much it differs from its historical average.

My proposed signal would be a relative-performance difference greater than two standard deviations from its historical average. I would investigate whether buying the relatively underperforming stock and shorting the relatively outperforming stock produces positive returns when the difference narrows.

I would also test whether the relationship remains stable over different market conditions.

6. Validation and risks

I would compare the strategy against buy-and-hold Ferrari, buy-and-hold Porsche, and an equally weighted portfolio of both stocks.

I would reject the idea if the relationship between the stocks is unstable or if the differences do not regularly reverse.

Potential risks include differences in business models, permanent changes in company valuations, short-selling costs, currency differences, and transaction costs. The strategy may also fail if one company consistently outperforms the other.


# Question 3: [Exploratory] Research Idea Number Three

## Answer:

### Title: Can Global Luxury Car Demand and Vehicle Deliveries Predict Sports Car Stock Performance?

1. Market and scope

I want to study publicly traded luxury sports car manufacturers, mainly Ferrari (NYSE: RACE) and Porsche AG (XETRA: P911). The research will focus on the global luxury automobile market, particularly the United States, Europe, and China. My intended investment horizon is 3–12 months.

2. Investment thesis

My hypothesis is that changes in vehicle deliveries and customer demand may provide useful information about future stock performance.

For example, if Ferrari reports increasing vehicle deliveries while Porsche reports declining deliveries, this could indicate differences in company demand and business performance.

I want to investigate whether changes in vehicle deliveries can help identify which company's stock may perform better in the following months.

3. Potential edge

Investors often focus on revenue and earnings when evaluating automobile companies. However, vehicle delivery numbers can provide additional information about actual customer demand.

My potential edge would be identifying changes in vehicle deliveries before they become fully reflected in financial results and stock prices.

I would also investigate whether demand in different geographic regions provides additional information about future company performance.

4. Data and sources

I would use these free resources:
- Ferrari Investor Relations: Official quarterly and annual reports containing vehicle shipment and financial information.
- Porsche Investor Relations: Official reports containing vehicle delivery figures and regional performance.
- Stooq: Historical stock prices for Ferrari and Porsche.
- Google Sheets: To organize and compare vehicle delivery figures and stock returns.
These sources are publicly accessible. I would need to verify the availability and consistency of historical delivery figures before starting the research.

5. Research approach

I would:
1. Collect quarterly vehicle delivery figures for Ferrari and Porsche.
2. Calculate the percentage change in deliveries compared with the previous quarter and the same quarter of the previous year.
3. Collect historical stock prices for both companies.
4. Compare delivery growth with stock performance over the following 3–12 months.
5. Investigate whether increasing deliveries are associated with stronger future stock returns.
My proposed signal would be consistent year-over-year growth in vehicle deliveries. I would investigate whether companies with stronger delivery growth outperform their competitors.

6. Validation and risks

I would compare the strategy against a simple buy-and-hold strategy for Ferrari and Porsche.

I would reject the idea if vehicle delivery growth does not consistently provide useful information about future stock performance.

Potential risks include:
- Higher deliveries do not necessarily mean higher profits.
- New vehicle launches may temporarily increase deliveries.
- Companies may have different production capacities.
- Delivery figures may already be reflected in stock prices.
- Quarterly reporting creates information delays.
- Currency differences and transaction costs may affect investment returns.
The purpose of this research is to determine whether vehicle delivery growth provides useful information for investment decisions, not to assume that higher deliveries automatically produce higher stock returns.
